Having Opus 5.5 operate Blender to build a fictional study Most of what I'm doing is written in the quoted post; this is the continuation. I added refinement passes, made the setup more detailed, and upped the detail to match. [What I learned] ① Requirements definition really is the most important thing Once the grade of the apartment is decided, the room's skeleton can be built. Once the structural type is decided, the wall thickness is decided, and so is how the window sashes fit (the AI can decide these). ② Context about my own history is powerful For the character living there (this time, me), it references a context file I wrote about my own history. As a result, it made things like the Noh masks my grandfather carved, a ceramic mug my grandmother fired, card games, and a tennis racket, without being told to. Data about your own history is super valuable. ③ Object creation needs a decision In the end I organized it into three tiers: AI builds it in Blender (bpy) / generate with Tripo HD / generate with Tripo SM. I had already worked out criteria for HD vs. SM before, so I had it choose based on observing the object and how likely its shape is to need editing. ④ Apply physics to every object How to eliminate placements that ignore physics, like objects intersecting each other or floating. I had it catalogue how every object is placed and inspect every single one, trying to reduce anything unnatural. It still needs polishing, but I plan to turn it into a Skill and reuse it. ⑤ The strongest rule is still "stop doing it your own way" I hammered this home again this time. It's even written in the AGENTS.md for my Blender work. Things like "For window sashes, look at a catalog, Fuji Sash or Sankyo, whichever." That said, ideally you'd first generalize specs from catalogs so they can be reused. (Or rather, manufacturers should make generalized scripts for us.) ⑥ Paying attention to motion seems good I'm a CG beginner, so what helped me most was @hayashimon1's technique of "putting motion into the scene." I made small things move a little, like a monitor screen that changes as the AI reasons, or a mini fan that spins. ⑦ Replace the background with an image This is a technique I learned from @H_Y_per. For the distant view, I used a generated image. No way I'm building a city model… This time it's an apartment, so the floor and height could stay vague, but for the interior of a low-rise building, managing the vanishing point coordinates would probably matter. ⑧ Little touches The books on the bookshelf are the note articles I've written so far. Stefan 3D AI@Stefan_3D_AI · 3 Oct 2026
Day 18. Building the game I've dreamed of for 15 years with AI. I've learned how to build proper levels with AI. I take the design and the architecture, and AI helps me put it all together and check the balance. I made the first level of my game almost entirely by hand. I wanted to understand how it works and what I like, from how big the trees should be to how the textures tile. After a day digging around in the terrain tools it all made sense, and I could see how much of it could be automated by painting zones. Claude helped me a lot with the tileable textures. Matching the colors took a ton of iterations, and that was the hardest part of the whole level. For level two I took a different route. I built myself a simple HTML planner with a logical map of the level, where I place everything and design the layout. Painting a level and making it fun to play are two different jobs, so I kept the design for myself. We even made automated tests with Claude: 2D simulations that show which routes a player takes and how hard the level is. They're rough, but they give a real sense of how the level plays. Once the plan was ready, I handed everything to Claude, and 3-4 hours later I had a working level. It reused the patterns from my first level, scattered objects unevenly so nothing looks placed on a grid, and generated extra props whenever I asked. I showed it one example, it picked up what worked, and we turned that into a skill for the next levels. The video is a quick flight over level two, then how it got there, from my first sketch to the version I play now.
